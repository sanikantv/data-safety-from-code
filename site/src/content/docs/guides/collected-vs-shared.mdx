**Short answer:** data is **collected** when it leaves the user's device, whether it goes to you
or to anyone else, including through an SDK. It is **shared** when data from your app is
transferred to a third party. A service provider working for you is not a third party, so
sending data to one is collection only. For each data type, the form asks whether it is
collected, shared, or both.

## Collected

Google defines collection as transmitting data off the user's device. That includes data sent
by libraries and SDKs in your app.

Not collection:

- Data processed only on the device, such as a local database or on-device processing.
- Data sent with end-to-end encryption that no one in between, you included, can read.

Reading data with a permission doesn't make it collected either. It becomes collected when the
data, or something derived from it, leaves the device.

## Shared

Google defines sharing as transferring user data collected from your app to a third party. That
covers server-to-server transfers, on-device transfers to another app, transfers through SDKs,
and transfers through a WebView your app controls. A third party is any organization other than
you (the developer publishing the app) and your service providers.

Google lists these transfers as **not sharing**:

| Exception | Example |
|---|---|
| A **service provider** that processes data on your behalf, under your instructions | Your cloud host, a crash reporter or a backend service you pay to store data |
| A **legal** purpose | A response to a valid legal request |
| A **user-initiated** action the user reasonably expects, or sharing after a prominent in-app disclosure and consent that meets Google's User Data policy | The user picks another app to send a photo to |
| Fully **anonymized** data that can no longer be linked to a person | Aggregated counts with no identifiers |

Google's page also points out that anonymizing data properly is hard, and suggests consulting
privacy and security experts. Source:
[Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469).

## How common SDKs fit

What follows is how each vendor's disclosure is recorded in this project's SDK knowledge base.
Always check the vendor page itself.

- **[Firebase Analytics](/sdk/firebase-analytics/)** and
  **[Firebase Crashlytics](/sdk/firebase-crashlytics/)**: collected, not shared. The Analytics
  entry notes that linking Google Ads, Google Signals or data sharing settings can change what
  is collected and whether it counts as shared, so review those settings.
- **[AdMob](/sdk/admob/)**: Google's disclosure lists approximate location, app interactions,
  diagnostics and device or other IDs as collected **and** shared.
- **[RevenueCat](/sdk/revenuecat/)**: purchase history is collected, not shared.
- **[Facebook SDK](/sdk/facebook-sdk/)** app events: recorded as shared. That entry is inferred
  from Meta's documentation, not a Play-specific disclosure.

## CSV IDs

For each data type, the "collected, shared, or both" question is a multiple-choice question.
Select both rows for "both".

- `PSL_DATA_USAGE_ONLY_COLLECTED`: the data is collected.
- `PSL_DATA_USAGE_ONLY_SHARED`: the data is shared.
- The purposes for collection go in the `DATA_USAGE_COLLECTION_PURPOSE` rows and the purposes
  for sharing in the `DATA_USAGE_SHARING_PURPOSE` rows, using IDs such as
  `PSL_APP_FUNCTIONALITY`, `PSL_ANALYTICS` and `PSL_ADVERTISING`.

For approximate location the full question ID is
`PSL_DATA_USAGE_RESPONSES:PSL_APPROX_LOCATION:PSL_DATA_USAGE_COLLECTION_AND_SHARING`.

## Common mistakes

- **Marking every SDK as shared.** A vendor that processes data only for you is a service
  provider. Over-declaring sharing makes your listing look worse than it is.
- **Marking an ad network as collected only.** Ad networks often use the data for their own
  purposes as well. Read the vendor's disclosure.
- **Forgetting your backend.** If your server passes user data to another company, that is
  sharing, even though no SDK is involved. Code in the app can't show this.
- **Filling sharing purposes for data that isn't shared**, or the reverse.
