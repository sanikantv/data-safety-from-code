**Short answer:** a flagged or rejected Data safety form usually means Google saw the app
do something the form doesn't declare. The usual source is an SDK: an ads, analytics or crash
library sending data you didn't list. Read the exact issue in Play Console, find the code or
SDK behind it, then either declare the data or remove what sends it, and submit again.

## What Google says

From Google's [Data safety help page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469):

- You alone are responsible for complete and accurate declarations.
- If Google finds a discrepancy between the app's behavior and the form, it may take
  enforcement action.
- Apps that don't become compliant can have updates blocked or be removed from Google Play.
- Google tells you about issues by email to the account owner, a Play Console notification,
  and the **Policy status** page (Policy, then Policy status).
- Google's review of the form is not designed to verify that every answer is accurate. A form
  that passes review can still be wrong.

## Steps to fix it

1. **Read the issue.** Open **Policy → Policy status** and note exactly what it names: a data
   type, an SDK, a permission or a version code.
2. **List every SDK in the release build**, including ones pulled in by other libraries. Running
   `./gradlew :app:dependencies --configuration releaseRuntimeClasspath` shows the full tree.
3. **Check each SDK's disclosure.** Google points developers to their SDK providers' guidance and
   to the Google Play SDK Index. The [SDK pages](/sdk/) here link the vendor pages for common
   SDKs.
4. **Check your own code** for anything that sends data off the device: HTTP calls, uploads,
   database writes, analytics events, sign-in, AI requests.
5. **Check the merged manifest** for permissions libraries add. An unused sensitive permission
   is better removed than declared.
6. **Decide for each finding:** declare it, or remove the code or SDK that sends it. If you
   remove code, ship a new build. Older versions still distributed on Google Play count toward
   the form.
7. **Export a backup, update the form, check the preview, and submit.**

## Common causes

These follow from Google's rules. Each one is a gap between what the app does and what the form
says.

- **SDK data left out.** For example, AdMob's disclosure lists device or other IDs, app
  interactions, diagnostics and approximate location. Google's page says an Android
  Advertising ID is declared as device or other IDs.
- **Location derived from an IP address** but no location declared. See
  [IP address and location](/guides/ip-address-location/).
- **"No data collected"** while the app includes an analytics, crash or ads SDK. See
  [apps that collect no data](/guides/app-with-no-data/).
- **Crash logs and diagnostics** from a crash reporter left out. Google's page notes that
  diagnostics collected together with a device ID still need "Diagnostics" declared.
- **Encrypted in transit: yes**, with a plain `http://` endpoint receiving user data.
- **Purchase history** missing when your app or backend stores or verifies purchases.
- **An older version** still on Google Play that collects something the latest one doesn't.

## CSV IDs that often need adding

- `PSL_DEVICE_ID` (Device or other IDs)
- `PSL_APPROX_LOCATION` (Approximate location)
- `PSL_USER_INTERACTION` (App interactions)
- `PSL_CRASH_LOGS` and `PSL_PERFORMANCE_DIAGNOSTICS` (Crash logs, Diagnostics)
- `PSL_PURCHASE_HISTORY` (Purchase history)
- `PSL_DATA_COLLECTION_ENCRYPTED_IN_TRANSIT` (the encryption question)

Each added type also needs its usage rows: collected or shared, ephemeral, required or optional,
and purposes. See the [CSV format reference](/reference/csv-format/).

## Common mistakes when fixing

- **Fixing only the item named in the notice.** If one SDK was missed, others may be too. Do the
  full inventory.
- **Resubmitting without changes**, hoping for a different reviewer.
- **Declaring everything "just in case".** Over-declaring makes the listing inaccurate in the
  other direction. Declare what the app and its SDKs actually do.
- **Importing a CSV without a backup.** Import replaces the whole form.
